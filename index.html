Welcome to The Self‑Sufficiency Club

Welcome to this little corner of the internet — a space built for everyone. Able‑bodied, broken‑bodied, young, old, tired, wired, alien, confused, curious… whoever you are, you’re welcome here. If you’ve ever wanted to live a little more self‑sufficiently, or even just learn one new skill that makes life feel steadier, you’ve found your people.

My husband and I live on a small farm where we raise our own beefies — and yes, we slaughter and process them ourselves. We fish regularly to feed our family. We make our own cheese from the milk we produce on‑farm. We preserve, we cook, we hunt, we learn, and we fail forward. None of it is fancy. All of it is real.

I also live with SLE (lupus), which means I’m physically limited in ways that most gardening guides don’t account for. I can’t spend long in the sun. I can’t bend or lift the way I used to. Some days my body simply says “nope.” But the need to grow food — to be connected to my land, my animals, and my pantry — never went away.

So I started inventing ways to keep going. I grow food in drums, because they’re tall enough that I don’t have to bend, and portable enough that I can move them into shade when my skin can’t handle the UV. I’ve learned how to adapt tools, change layouts, and rethink the “traditional” garden so it works for a body that doesn’t always cooperate. And I want to share all of that with you.

This blog will become a home for accessible gardening, seasonal growing guides for New Zealand, cooking lessons & recipes, cheese‑making, preserving, hunting, fishing, wild food gathering, and real stories from our farm — the wins, the fails, and the “well that was unexpected” moments.

Whether you’re here to grow one tomato plant or build a full homestead — you belong here.
Let’s grow what we can, in the bodies we have, with the land we’re lucky enough to stand on.
