What to Start Planting Right Now in New Zealand — Accessible Drum Gardening Edition

Late August and early September in New Zealand is that exciting shoulder-season moment: winter is loosening its grip, the soil is waking up, and everything is itching to grow again. But if you’re like me — living with SLE, limited sun time, limited bending, lifting, or long days outside — traditional gardening setups aren’t always realistic.

That’s why I garden in drums. They’re tall, portable, warm up faster than the ground, and let you grow real food without needing to kneel, bend, or spend hours in the sun. Here’s what you can start planting or prepping right now in New Zealand.

1. Early Spring Greens (Perfect for Drums)

These thrive in cooler temps and don’t mind the tail end of winter. Plant spinach, silverbeet, lettuce mixes, rocket, mizuna, and kale. Greens love consistent moisture, and drums drain beautifully. Plus, you can move them into shade when the sun gets too harsh.

2. Root Veggies You Can Start Now

Late winter is still great for cool-season roots like carrots, beetroot, radishes, and spring onions. Use deep drums for carrots and beetroot. The height prevents carrot fly, and the loose drum soil helps roots grow straight.

3. Herbs That Love Early Spring

Herbs like parsley, coriander, chives, and mint thrive in early spring. Herbs stay cleaner, drier, and easier to harvest when elevated in drums.

4. Strawberries — Prep Now, Plant Soon

Late August is the perfect time to prep your drum for strawberries. Add compost, sheep pellets, or blood & bone. Plant runners or new plants in early September. Drum sides keep slugs and snails away.

5. Potatoes — Yes, You Can Grow Them in Drums

Potatoes are one of the easiest accessible crops. Add soil to the bottom, place seed potatoes, cover lightly, and add more soil as they grow. Early varieties like Swift, Jersey Benne, or Rocket are perfect right now.

6. Prep Drums for Warm-Season Crops

You can’t plant tomatoes, cucumbers, or beans outdoors yet — NZ nights are still too cold — but you can prep your drums now. Add compost, slow-release fertiliser, mulch, and position drums where they’ll get good sun later.

7. Seed Starting Indoors

If you can’t be outside long, start seeds inside. Begin tomatoes, capsicums, basil, cucumbers, and zucchini indoors now. Move them into drums in late October when frosts are gone.

A Note for My Fellow Broken-Bodied Gardeners

You don’t need perfect health to grow food. You don’t need a big garden. You don’t need long days in the sun. You just need a drum, some good soil, a few seeds, and the willingness to adapt. I grow because it feeds my family, my pantry, and my spirit — even on the days my body doesn’t cooperate. And if you’re here reading this, you can grow too.
